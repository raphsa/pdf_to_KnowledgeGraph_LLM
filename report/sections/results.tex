\section{Results and Discussions}

The outcomes of this project demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of transforming a complex, domain-specific report into a structured, machine-interpretable knowledge base. Starting from the Sustainable Health from Food – National Nutrition Recommendations 2024 report \cite{thl2024nutrition}, the developed pipeline successfully extracted, organized, and operationalized knowledge for downstream use in language model grounding and evaluation.

The automated PDF ingestion and parsing process produced a well-structured dataset combining narrative and tabular elements. By leveraging the table of contents to preserve document hierarchy, the system maintained contextual integrity across sections while avoiding the fragmentation typically caused by page based extraction. The resulting dataset provided a faithful digital representation of the original report, forming a strong foundation for subsequent analytical stages.

The design of the domain ontology proved essential in organizing the extracted information into meaningful categories. Defining entities such as ingredient, nutrient, dietary guideline, and health outcome allowed the project to bridge textual information with formal semantic structure. This ontology not only guided the entity and relation extraction phases but also ensured conceptual consistency.

Entity and relation extraction yielded a good representation of the report’s contents. Thousands of entity mentions were mapped into typed relations. Although some ambiguity remained around overlapping or generic terms, the overall comprehensiveness of extracted relations was good, and this showed how also policy-oriented reports can be formalized into RDF-style triples. The resulting knowledge graph allowed queries that connect, for instance, food items with both their nutritional properties and associated health outcomes, thus offering systematic way to explore dietary recommendations.

Based on the structured knowledge graph, the generation of grounded instruction–response pairs provided a natural language layer that connected formal data with conversational tasks. This dataset demonstrated how triples could be rephrased into usable prompts and answers for LLM and evaluation. The inclusion of grounding metadata, such as source page numbers, strengthened transparency and interpretability.

Starting from the instruction–response dataset, the performance of the large language model (LLM) fine-tuned on the dataset’s contextual information was analyzed and compared with the two baseline models: a context-free LLM and a RAG model enriched through the developed knowledge graph.
The evaluation revealed the strong performance of the fine-tuned model, which, despite being trained on a single source, demonstrated the ability to effectively learn and reproduce the topic contained in the source PDF. As detailed in the evaluation section, the outcomes of the automatic metrics were primarily influenced by the output format generated by the three models, favoring the fine-tuned LLM, whose responses exhibited a stylistic coherence with those of the instruction–response dataset.
The most noteworthy findings emerged from the rubric-based metrics, which enabled a more precise assessment of the substantive quality of the responses. These results indicated that the fine-tuned model achieved the highest score in Citation Correctness (Fig.~\ref{fig:citation}) and performed well in Relevance (Fig.~\ref{fig:relevance}). Conversely, the presence of highly concise responses in the instruction–response dataset led the fine-tuned model to adopt a similarly succinct style, which ultimately penalized its performance in the Completeness metric (Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}).

Finally, the interactive demo illustrated the end-to-end usability of the system. By allowing users to ask natural language questions and view the model’s grounded answers alongside their document citations, the interface made the abstract process of knowledge extraction and retrieval tangible. This proved valuable not only as a demonstration tool but also as a validation mechanism, showing that domain-grounded AI can serve as a bridge between expert reports and public-facing, interpretable systems.

Overall, the results highlight the potential of integrating structured knowledge extraction, semantic modelling, and grounded language generation in complex expert domains. The project illustrates how authoritative but static documents can be converted into dynamic information systems, ultimately contributing to more transparent and accountable applications of AI in health and nutrition.
