\section{Introduction}

Knowledge representation in structured form has become an increasingly important pillar in the development of intelligent systems. A key paradigm is the Knowledge Graph (KG), which represents real-world entities as nodes and the relationships between them as edges, enabling both human-readable and machine-processable semantics. Parallel to this, the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) \cite{llmdefinition} has transformed how unstructured textual data is used and processed \cite{llm_impact}. However, while LLMs excel at language understanding, they are prone to producing content that is not grounded in factual evidence, commonly termed \textit{hallucinations} \cite{2023survey}. Integrating KGs with LLMs is a promising direction for mitigating these errors, as KGs can provide an external knowledge source to ground LLM outputs and reduce unanchored generation \cite{unifyingllmkg}.

Despite these advancements, the primary source of high-value domain knowledge often remains documents in PDF format. Extracting structured knowledge from these documents poses substantial technical challenges: the text is frequently unstructured and interspersed with complex layouts containing tables, figures, hierarchical headings and embedded images \cite{challengesExtract}. In the food domain specifically, converting such documents into structured formats is critical for enabling ``food intelligence'' and interoperable systems \cite{foodrecognition}.

In this context, this project addresses the following research problem: \textit{how to extract structured knowledge from a domain-specific PDF (in the food domain), construct a Knowledge Graph (KG) and use that graph to generate grounded instruction--response pairs for LLM fine-tuning.} We further aim to systematically compare this fine-tuned model against two robust baselines: (i) zero-shot LLM \cite{llmzeroshot} and (ii) Retrieval-Augmented Generation over the KG (RAG-KG) \cite{whatRAG} \cite{RAGsurvey}. This comparative approach is motivated by recent literature debating the efficacy of fine-tuning versus RAG, where studies suggest that while RAG excels in retrieving dynamic knowledge, fine-tuning can be superior for learning specific tasks or formats \cite{finetuning}.

The specific domain of application for this project is sustainable nutrition. Recent dietary guidelines, such as the Finnish ``National Nutrition Recommendations 2024'' \cite{thl2024nutrition} and the broader Nordic Nutrition Recommendations \cite{blomhoff2023nordic}, emphasize not only individual health but also the planetary health consequences of food production and consumption. For instance, food consumption in Finland is estimated to account for approximately 20\% of the annual climate impacts of the average consumer and diets aligned with nutritional and sustainability goals can potentially reduce this climate footprint by over one-third \cite{thl2024nutrition}.

As the main source of domain knowledge, this project relies on the report \textit{Sustainable Health from Food -- National Nutrition Recommendations 2024} by the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare \cite{thl2024nutrition}. This document provides an evidence-based synthesis of food and health recommendations, yet it exists primarily as a human-readable PDF, where valuable knowledge is distributed across unstructured sections, tables and figures. By converting this complex PDF into structured knowledge, a pipeline was established that enables downstream tasks, such as fact-based instruction–response generation, and allows for a systematic evaluation of grounded versus ungrounded model settings.
